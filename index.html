Why does this problem matter?
Three real-world scenarios illustrate what "78% unstructured" means in practice and why it blocks Tempus's commercial potential.

Pharma Partner: Trial Matching Failure

We need all T2DM patients on metformin with HbA1c above 65 who have NOT been prescribed SGLT2 inhibitors.
❌ Problem: 78% of GP records are free text. A SQL query returns nothing. A human must read 500,000 notes manually or the trial goes unfilled.

Insights Licensing: Data Gap

AstraZeneca wants to license a longitudinal T2DM cohort: diagnosis date, HbA1c trajectory, medication history, complications.
❌ Problem: The GP record holds all of this but as unstructured text. Tempus cannot build the cohort, losing a premium licensing deal.

FUSES Model Training: Data Starvation

Tempus's FUSES Foundation Model needs more cross-modal training data beyond oncology to expand into cardiology and metabolic disease.
❌ Problem: GP chronic disease notes are the richest longitudinal source but unstructured text can't be used as model training data.

The PoC Solution: NLP for GP T2DM Records

This project designs and pilots a ClinicalBERT-powered NLP pipeline that automatically extracts structured clinical entities from free-text GP notes (diagnoses, medications, lab values, dates) and maps them to OMOP CDM v5.4. The PoC uses the Deep 6 AI ingestion infrastructure already in place within Tempus's group architecture.


Focus condition: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) high commercial value, consistent clinical vocabulary, available annotation benchmarks.

Connection to Group Assignment

The Group Assignment designed a 6-layer Medallion Data Lakehouse for Tempus AI with an Agentic NLP Abstraction Engine as Objective 2. This PoC operationalises Layers 1-4 of that architecture (Bronze ingestion → Silver harmonisation → NLP engine → Gold serving) for one specific sub-corpus (GP T2DM notes from the Deep 6 dataset) validating the approach before enterprise-scale rollout.

From China to Ireland: Same Problem, Different Rules
China has already proven that structuring primary care data at scale is technically feasible. Ireland faces the same challenge, but under GDPR, a voluntary GP system, and a very different starting point.

Why China's approach cannot be directly transposed

Three things make Ireland's context fundamentally different:

(1) GDPR Article 17 right to erasure means all derived structured records must be deletable on patient request, requiring Delta Lake DELETE operations designed in advance, not retrospectively.

(2) Voluntary participation means health systems must be convinced, not mandated, trust takes longer to build.

(3) Irish-English GP shorthand ("T2DM x14 yrs", "HbA1c 58 - stable", "review 3/12") is entirely different from the Mandarin clinical text that Chinese NLP models were trained on. ClinicalBERT, fine-tuned on English clinical notes, is the right starting point, but GP-specific fine-tuning is still required.

Ireland's digital health moment - 2026

In February 2026, the Irish government approved procurement for a National Electronic Health Record, the first concrete step towards a unified system, arriving 7-8 years later than most comparable EU countries. The Health Information Bill 2024 introduces a statutory duty to share health information. This creates the regulatory environment in which a Tempus GP NLP PoC becomes both commercially viable and strategically timely.

How the technology works - three everyday analogies
Clinical NLP can seem opaque. These three mappings show exactly what each component does and why it is designed the way it is.
① NER (Named Entity Recognition): The Parcel Sorter
Parcel sorting machine
"To: John Smith, 24 Grafton St, Dublin 2"
→ recognises: person · street address · postal zone
same logic→
ClinicalBERT NER model
"metformin 500 BD, HbA1c 72, no nephropathy"
→ recognises: drug · lab value · negated diagnosis

The model doesn't "understand" medicine. It has learned from millions of annotated clinical notes to recognise which words are drug names, which numbers are lab values, and critically which diagnoses are negated ("no nephropathy noted" should NOT be recorded as a nephropathy diagnosis).

② OMOP CDM: The Currency Exchange
Currency exchange
USD / EUR / GBP - different forms, same value.
Exchange rate converts all to one standard.
same logic→
OMOP CDM v5.4
"metformin 500 BD" / "Metformin 500mg BID" / "met 500 twice"
→ all map to RxNorm concept 860975

Without this standardisation, a query for "patients on metformin" fails across records from different GP practices. OMOP CDM is the universal vocabulary that makes cross-site, cross-acquisition queries possible and is the same standard used in the Group Assignment's Silver layer.

③ Confidence Scoring: The Airport Security Queue
Airport security scanner
Most bags pass automatically.
Ambiguous → human officer checks.
Clear problem → separate handling.
same logic→
NER confidence routing
Confidence ≥ 0.85 → auto-promote to Silver.
Confidence < 0.85 → human review queue.
Human corrections → model retraining.

Human reviewers are not a fallback for failure, they are an integral part of the active learning loop. Every correction feeds back into monthly ClinicalBERT retraining cycles, making the model progressively better at Irish GP text specifically.

Anticipated Challenges & Risk Mitigation
Critical evaluation of the real risks in this PoC, not dismissed, but addressed.
RiskLevelWhy it mattersMitigation
Model domain mismatch HIGH ClinicalBERT trained on hospital notes (MIMIC-III); GP text is shorter, more colloquial, abbreviation-heavy. F1 may be significantly lower than benchmarks suggest. Conservative 0.85 confidence threshold; GP-specific annotation sprint; if F1 <0.78, pause for fine-tuning before proceeding.
GDPR vs HIPAA complexity HIGH EU patient data requires documented lawful basis, data minimisation, right to erasure. Group architecture designed for HIPAA; GDPR adds requirements HIPAA doesn't have. Data Processing Agreements with originating health systems; Delta Lake DELETE for erasure rights; all processing in Azure EU West; Microsoft Azure DPA covers SCCs.
Ground truth scarcity MED 5,000 annotated GP records needed for evaluation. Clinician annotation is expensive; ICD codes as proxy labels introduce noise. Dedicated GP-trained annotators; inter-annotator agreement ≥0.80 (Cohen's Kappa) required before using as gold standard.
Human review queue overload MED If >20% of records fall below 0.85 threshold, reviewer backlog and fatigue create operational bottleneck and degrade active learning feedback loop. Monthly per-entity precision reporting from week 1; entity types with >20% review rate trigger targeted fine-tuning sprint.
FUSES incompatibility MED Group architecture used FUSES Encoder as primary NLP model, but FUSES has no published benchmarks on non-oncology GP records. Use ClinicalBERT as primary; validate FUSES on 50,000 annotated GP records before substitution.
Clinician adoption resistance LOW NLP errors in clinical data have downstream consequences; clinicians may resist if trust is not established early. All outputs labelled "NLP-derived, subject to clinical review". Monthly per-entity precision reporting cadence.

The core problem

Ireland has no national diabetes registry and no structured electronic diabetes record. As Diabetes Ireland (2023) states: "with no data - no diabetes registry nor clinical audits - we cannot even fully identify what the gaps and issues are." Without structured GP records, the HSE cannot identify poorly controlled patients, target preventive interventions, or measure whether care improvements are working. This is precisely what the NLP pipeline addresses.
